"ABD" — all but dissertation — is common enough in academia to have its
own acronym, which should tell you something: this isn't a rare failure
mode, it's a well-known, permanent address that a huge number of
otherwise-finished PhD students live at indefinitely. The Council of
Graduate Schools tracked nearly 49,000 students across 30 institutions
and found that ten years after starting, only about 56.6% had completed
their doctorate. Not "hadn't finished yet" — many of that remainder never
will. These aren't people who failed coursework or bombed their
qualifying exams. By the time you're ABD you've cleared every gate
someone else was watching.

That's the specific thing that makes this last mile expensive, and it's
a different mechanism from either of the last two weeks. It isn't
compounding technical debt (software) and it isn't geometry (broadband).
It's that every earlier stage of a PhD has an external forcing function
— a course has a due date, a qualifying exam has a scheduled sitting,
your advisor is in the room — and the dissertation is the first
deliverable in years of study that doesn't. Nobody is refusing to let you
into the next room until it's done. There's no room. You're already
qualified to call yourself ABD, which means the label itself removes the
pressure that got you this far.

This is a complication of the thesis worth taking seriously: it predicts
that the fix for procrastination-driven last miles isn't more talent or
more discipline, it's re-introducing the thing that was missing —
external structure. Programs that build in staged, scheduled
dissertation milestones (chapter defenses, committee check-ins with
actual calendar dates) report meaningfully better completion than
programs that hand a student a topic and see them again at submission.
The lesson generalizes past academia: whenever a last mile is stalling
not because it's technically hard but because nobody else is waiting on
it, the fix isn't to work harder at finishing — it's to manufacture the
external deadline that the rest of the project had for free.
