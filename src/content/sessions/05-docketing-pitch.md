Groups of three. You're a small legal-practice team pitching a redesigned
deadline-tracking (docketing) system to the partners — played by the rest
of the lab — who are skeptical of spending money on "paperwork
infrastructure" instead of billable work.

## Before the lab

Read week 5's lecture and at least skim the malpractice study it cites.
Your pitch needs a real failure mode to react to, and that study is full of
them.

## In the lab

Five minutes per group, then two minutes of partner questions. A strong
pitch names the exact way deadlines currently get missed (a single point of
failure, no redundant check, dates buried in email) and proposes something
structurally different, not just "try harder" — per the lecture's own
argument, trying harder is exactly the response that doesn't work against a
step-function failure.

## Submitting

The slide deck or one-pager used in the pitch, from the group, annotated
afterwards with the two toughest partner questions you got and how you'd
answer them with more time. Due Friday 26 March, 5pm.
