Groups of three. Prepare and deliver a three-minute spoken safety briefing
— the kind a cabin crew or airline safety office might actually give —
that accurately explains why final approach and landing carry
disproportionate accident risk, using the real statistics week 10's
lecture and its linked sources cite.

## Before the lab

Read week 10's lecture and skim the Boeing and Flight Safety Foundation
material it links. Your numbers need to be real; a briefing that
exaggerates risk for effect fails its own brief.

## In the lab

Draft and rehearse as a group, then deliver to the room (or record and
play back, if a group needs a retake). A strong briefing explains the
"why" in one sentence a nervous passenger could actually follow — most of
a flight is stable and instrumented, and the last few minutes concentrate
manual judgement calls, weather, and traffic into a much smaller window.

## Submitting

The script or speaking notes used, from the group, plus a recording or live
delivery in the lab. Due Friday 14 May, 5pm.

Groups of three.
