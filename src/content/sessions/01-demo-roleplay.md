Pairs. In each pair, one person demos a piece of software they've actually
built (a uni project, a personal repo, anything with a UI you can click
through) as though it were finished. The other plays a skeptical product
manager whose entire job this lab is to not be satisfied — asking what
happens on a bad network, an empty state, a second user, a typo in the
input field. If you don't have your own project handy, swap: demo a
classmate's.

## Before the lab

Bring something you can actually run and click through — rough is fine,
"finished" is not required and is, honestly, the wrong thing to bring to a
lab about the gap between the two. Read week 1's lecture first; the PM's
whole brief is to go looking for exactly the gap it describes.

## In the lab

Ten minutes to demo, ten minutes of PM interrogation, then switch roles and
do it again with the other person's project. The PM's job isn't to be
mean — it's to make the specific, nameable case for why "it works" and "it
works for someone who isn't you, watching" are different claims.

## Submitting

One page, from the pair: the gaps the PM actually found, and — this is the
part markers care about — which of them the developer says they'd
genuinely missed if nobody had asked. Due Friday 26 February, 5pm.

Work in pairs of two for this one.
