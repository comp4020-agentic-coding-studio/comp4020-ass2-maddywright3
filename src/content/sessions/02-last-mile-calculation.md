You're given a rollout brief in the lab: a fictional broadband or rail
extension with a customer density map, a per-kilometre trenching cost, and
a target coverage percentage. The job is to calculate the marginal cost of
connecting each additional 10% of customers, not just the total project
cost — the total tells you nothing about where the money actually goes.

## Before the lab

Read week 2's lecture. Bring a calculator or a spreadsheet; this lab is
graded on the working, not just the final number.

## In the lab

Working in pairs, calculate marginal cost per customer-decile from 0% to
100% coverage using the density map provided. Plot it. Then write two or
three sentences arguing, from your own numbers, why the last decile costs
disproportionately more — and name the one assumption in the brief (route
geography, minimum trench depth, whatever it turns out to be) that, if
false, would flatten your curve instead of steepening it.

## Submitting

The worked calculation, the plot, and the justification paragraph, from the
pair. Due Friday 5 March, 5pm.

Pairs of two.
