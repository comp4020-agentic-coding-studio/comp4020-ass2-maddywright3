Twelve labs, one mark. Grading each of the twelve on its own would either
force twelve separate high-stakes deadlines onto a course whose entire
argument is that the last mile is where things break, or it would force us
to pretend a rough week doesn't happen. Neither is honest, so the scheme
instead does what a full semester should: it tolerates exactly two bad
weeks — sick, swamped, or just a lab you didn't click with — without asking
you to explain yourself, and it counts the other ten in full.

## What you submit, each week

Every lab page under [the labs tab](/sessions/) states its own artefact —
a diary entry, a calculation with justification, a zine, a pitch recording,
a debate script — and its own group size. Submit what that week's page
asks for, by that week's due date, through the same course drop-box used
for the two larger assessments.

## Why "best 10, drop 2" instead of a weighted average

A weighted average of twelve scores quietly punishes the two weeks you were
least prepared for exactly as hard as it rewards the ten you were most
prepared for — which is precisely the kind of unforgiving, no-recovery
curve week 5 spends its own lecture arguing is the wrong model for
low-stakes, frequent work. Dropping the worst two outright is the one
grading scheme that matches this course's own argument: it puts the "last
mile" pressure where it belongs, on actually turning up and doing the work
most weeks, and removes it from the one place it teaches nothing, which is
punishing a single bad week you can't retroactively fix.
