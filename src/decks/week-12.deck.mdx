# Eleven domains. One question left.

Which of these have you actually lived through?

---

## Week 1 — Software

Every shortcut you took to reach the demo gets paid back, with interest,
the moment it has to survive a real user.

---

## Week 2 — Infrastructure

The last customer on the line costs as much to connect as every other
customer put together.

---

## Week 3 — Graduate research

The dissertation is the one deliverable with no external deadline — which
is exactly why it's the easiest to keep deferring.

---

## Week 4 — Hospitality

A spotless bathroom earns zero new customers, which is exactly why it's
the first line item to get quietly shortchanged.

---

## Week 5 — Law

A case won on every substantive argument, destroyed completely by one
missed date on a calendar nobody was watching.

---

## Week 6 — Manufacturing

The same flaw costs roughly a hundred times more once the customer finds
it — measured, not metaphorical.

---

## Week 7 — Institutional handovers

Two teams each finish their half correctly, and the failure lives in the
seam neither team's own review was built to see.

---

## Week 8 — Startups

The finish line for "enough to raise" relocates exactly as fast as you
close the distance to it.

---

## Week 9 — Construction

Occupancy is a legal switch, not a percentage. 98% done buys a building
nothing.

---

## Week 10 — Aviation

The last five percent of a flight's duration carries roughly half its
historical fatal risk.

---

## Week 11 — Overbuilding

Past a point, continued finishing stops adding value to a thing and
starts subtracting it.

# It was never one mechanism

**It was one blind spot: in every domain, the people closest to the work
underestimated their own last mile — because their normal way of tracking
progress was never built to see that specific cost.**

```notes
This is the sentence the whole course has been building to. Say it once,
plainly, and stop talking. Let the quiz that follows on the lecture page
be where they answer it for themselves.
```
