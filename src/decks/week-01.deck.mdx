# Finishing is a different skill from building

**And it's the one almost everyone is worse at.**

```notes
Don't rush this slide. Let it sit. Everyone in the room has a half-finished
thing they're already thinking of — that's the point, not a distraction from it.
```

---

## Twelve weeks, twelve domains, one argument

Software. Infrastructure. Research. Hospitality. Law. Manufacturing.
Institutional handovers. Startups. Construction. Aviation. Open-source
overbuilding. And ourselves.

Different mechanisms. Same shape: the closer something gets to done, the
more the cost of the remaining distance stops matching the distance itself.

---

## Weeks 1–3

- **Week 1 — Software** — shipped demos that were never built to survive
  contact with a real user
- **Week 2 — Infrastructure** — the last customer on the line costs as much
  to connect as every other customer combined
- **Week 3 — Graduate research** — the dissertation is the one deliverable
  nobody else is waiting on, so it's the easiest to keep deferring

---

## Weeks 4–6

- **Week 4 — Hospitality** — the bathroom earns a restaurant zero new
  customers, which is exactly why it's the first thing to get shortchanged
- **Week 5 — Law** — a case won on every argument, destroyed completely by
  one missed date on a calendar nobody was watching
- **Week 6 — Manufacturing** — the same defect costs roughly a hundred
  times more once the customer finds it, and it's a measured number, not a
  vibe

---

## Weeks 7–9

- **Week 7 — Institutional handovers** — two teams each finish their half
  correctly, and the failure lives in the seam neither team's own review
  could see
- **Week 8 — Startups** — the finish line relocates exactly as fast as you
  close the distance to it
- **Week 9 — Construction** — occupancy is a legal switch, not a
  percentage; 98% done buys a building nothing

---

## Weeks 10–12

- **Week 10 — Aviation** — the last five percent of a flight's duration
  carries roughly half its historical fatal risk
- **Week 11 — Overbuilding** — past a point, continued finishing stops
  adding value and starts subtracting it
- **Week 12 — Synthesis** — eleven mechanisms, one shared blind spot

---

## How the assessment holds you to this

- **Labs — 20%** — twelve weekly chances to practise finishing something
  small, not just building it
- **Assignment 1 — 30%, week 6** — find something real and unfinished, and
  name precisely why *its* last mile is expensive
- **Final project — 50%, week 12** — go back and actually cross it

```notes
Land this on "actually cross it" — the whole course is set up so that
diagnosing a last mile (Assignment 1) is worth less than crossing one
(Final project). That's the argument made structural, not just stated.
```
